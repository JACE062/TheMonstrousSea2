## What the October audit established

- The whole voyage upgrade path costs **1,390g**: hull I→IV costs 850; three Mk I→III fittings cost 540. A difficulty-8 ordinary victory averages **1,127.5g**. The final Titan averages **4,970.5g** without monuments, explaining the player's 4,989g receipt directly.
- A player-facing purchase wrapper around CautiousBot reproduced the reported pacing: 33 of 36 runs reaching the Region 2 Haven left fully upgraded. One complete voyage ended with 19,776g after all upgrades and repairs. This is a transaction audit, not a human win-rate estimate: the bot completed only one of 600 starts.
- In the audited October baseline iron had one earning path: Search Hold at a wreck, 25% for one iron. No Titan, ordinary/Apex victory, merchant or ending grants it. Both later blueprints cost eight iron plus six barnacles. Even a survival-assumed full campaign deliberately prioritizing wrecks averages only **2.74 iron**; its chance of eight is about **0.44%**. Routing toward wrecks also reduces opportunities for barnacles.
- Stock simulators never temper fittings and do not upgrade hulls at Havens. Their ordinary reports cannot establish player upgrade timing. The audit uses actual purchases, actual paid repair access, and separate gross-income/spending ledgers.

## Limited material trade: accepted direction and open stock rules

**Established by the user, 31 October 2026:** Existing merchants should be able to sell meta-materials as well as consumables. This keeps a merchant useful when the belt is already stocked. A captain may risk current-voyage viability to buy permanent progress, or spend otherwise transient gold when expecting to lose the voyage. Supply must be strictly limited: a rich captain must not be able to buy an unrestricted quantity of one resource and cease pursuing it through other sources. These are design decisions only; material purchases are not implemented.

This acceptance concerns in-voyage gold spending. The user selected **Sea Glass and Depth-Wrought Iron** as the material sale catalog, providing variety and an alternative source for both. The later pre-Titan stock decision below does not establish that every other merchant carries both. Organic currencies, Titan trophies and other meta-resources are not part of this selected catalog. Material amounts follow existing permanent retention rules; the purchase idea does not bank unspent gold, commission a blueprint mid-voyage or change permanent unlock costs.

**Pre-Titan stock direction, 1 November 2026:** In response to the choice between reliably stocking both materials and varying which appear, the user selected reliable availability and suggested varying quantities instead. A fresh pre-Titan merchant should have both glass and iron available to purchase. Quantity variation is endorsed in principle, with a positive minimum for each material and a finite upper limit; exact ranges, distributions and prices remain unselected, while the later 4 November decision holds material stock levels even across regions. A zero initial quantity would undermine the chosen availability guarantee. This describes initial stock, not replenishment after buying it out. The decision applies to the pre-Titan specialist under discussion; the later ketch decision keeps smuggler stock chance-based, and both post-Titan stronghold shops now also have reliable limited glass/iron stocks selected.

**Independent merchant stock selected by the user, 4 November 2026 — not implemented:** Each distinct voyage merchant has its own wares, with random stock generation determining the eligible items present and how plentiful/rare its assortment is within bounded quantities. Material and consumable allocations remain separate. A new merchant brings a separate finite shipment rather than drawing purchases from a shared region/voyage quota.

Once rolled, that merchant's stock stays fixed for the encounter. Purchases deplete it; reopening or restoring a supported checkpoint does not refill or reroll it, including an empty material stock result. This does not authorize new save stops or select the technical timing/storage of generation. Balance total supply across all encountered merchants and salvage rather than adding an invisible global purchase limit.

Random variation preserves the already-selected merchant-type rules: pre-Titan specialists and both post-Titan stronghold merchants reliably start with positive bounded glass and iron stock; ketch material presence remains chance-based and may be absent. Consumable types/counts vary without guaranteeing a particular item. The user's rarity direction concerns the offered assortment; it does not select stronger consumable variants, new merchandise categories or new item-rarity mechanics. Material stock levels are now selected to remain even across regions. Exact stock distributions, quantity bounds and prices remain open.

The current [merchant lifecycle](../../packages/monstrous_core/lib/src/session/game_session.dart) already retains ordinary consumable stock when entering/leaving the stronghold shop. [Voyage checkpoints](../../packages/monstrous_core/lib/src/persistence/voyage_save_data.dart) serialize/restore that stock. This is implementation evidence for the existing shop behavior, not proof that future material purchases are persistence-safe. Wandering smuggler encounters remain unsupported save stops; no broader save phase is authorized here.

**Relative rarity/value direction accepted, 31 October 2026:** Following the comparison of rarity and price, the user endorsed using a higher iron price as the main distinction from glass, while preserving useful access and merchant variety. The later pre-Titan decision guarantees both material types there; variety can come from bounded quantities instead of their presence. Material and consumable stocks now have separate finite allocations, selected 3 November. Ketch material-stock chances, quantity ranges and prices remain open; both post-Titan stronghold shops now have reliable limited material stocks selected. Any frequency difference elsewhere should avoid recreating the bottleneck; extremely rare and prohibitively expensive iron is not the intended combination. No exact price, quantity range or appearance rate elsewhere is selected.

A visible purchase price makes the sacrifice of repair/refit money understandable; relying mainly on rare appearances makes progress depend on finding the stock at all. Pre-Titan quantity variation preserves some uncertainty while keeping the purpose of visiting dependable. It does not guarantee affordability or enough iron to finish a particular unlock at one visit. The user also accepted using shop purchases as a reference when comparing wreck outcomes: recovered iron can save a future material purchase, while a recovered Patch Kit can save repair spending. That is a comparison lens, not a fixed exchange rate or a new wreck reward. Revisit exact prices, amounts and stock-persistence implementation after encounter/service access is agreed, alongside total acquisition and competing purchases. The merchant direction remains part of the [wreck/hunt comparison](navigation.md#next-focused-discussion-one-wreck-and-one-hunt).

**Pre-Titan information selected, 1 November 2026:** Before choosing a course, the player should clearly distinguish the repair/refit destination from the merchant. Material quantities and the types of consumables in the merchant's stock must remain unknown until arrival. The established glass/iron availability is knowledge of the merchant's service, not an exact cargo preview. This decision is scoped to the pre-Titan preparation choice; the information supplied for ordinary sea encounters, any other competing course and the wider navigation format remains open. It does not select a fully revealed branching map.

When the encounter/service structure is agreed, budget material purchases alongside repairs, supplies and specialization. Choosing permanent progress can deliberately delay a voyage upgrade; the late-Region-3 full-upgrade target remains a successful-run target while paying repairs, not a promise to max upgrades and buy every meta-material on every route. Validate early deaths and first-Titan clears as well as full voyages. Limited purchases on a doomed run are intended; unlimited stock, duplicate awards or repeatedly refilled stock are not ways to realize that intent. No auto-purchase, new shop location, new currency or immediate implementation is selected.
