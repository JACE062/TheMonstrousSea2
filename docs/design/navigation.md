### Voyage purpose and navigation

The whole voyage is dangerous open sea. Ordinary monster hunting is the main activity and principal source of voyage spending money; wrecks and other discoveries offer strategic avoidance or tactical risk. Local refuge islands and shrines remain trustworthy safety, rather than introducing sheltered travel lanes or turning apparent respite into traps. Preserve exploration, imperfect information and viable cautious decisions.

Retain three current headings for ordinary choices, including repeated types and all-hunt sets. Ordinary hunts retain their creature/Apex tells under existing information restrictions. Wreck creatures remain unidentified until appearing; broad condition clues are usually readable from afar, with occasional inconclusive views. Neither exact cargo nor a future branching map is revealed.

| Region | Ordinary slots before / after midpoint | Optional midpoint | Final numbered preparation | Titan |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Siren Shoals | 4 / 4 | 5 | 10 | After slot 10 |
| Sargasso Drift | 5 / 5 | 6 | 12 | After slot 12 |
| Boiling Reach | 6 / 6 | 7 | 14 | After slot 14 |

The opening fight counts in Region 1's first four. Each midpoint offers one known Haven and two ordinary regional encounters; Nemesis cannot occupy this set. Choosing an ordinary alternative uses that midpoint slot. Final preparation offers exactly repair/refit versus merchant, not both visits. Post-Titan strongholds follow Regions 1 and 2 outside this numbered cadence. An ordinary wreck, its internal fight, or passing it empty each uses one slot.

### Wreck choices and complete rewards

| Condition, if approach has not triggered an ambush | Recovery decision | Cargo role |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Picked-clean | Quick small salvage; no deeper search | Modest remaining supplies/materials, possibly a few coins |
| Uncertain | Quick modest salvage and leave, or forego it and linger | Lingering offers richer cargo with a high fight chance |
| Fresh | Sail past empty, or commit to richer recovery | High fight chance; no quick small-salvage action |

Most cautious approaches permit assessment and empty withdrawal, but any condition can conceal an occasional forced ambush. Forced ambushes use normal-tier regional monsters only, even under Apex-chance modifiers. A voluntary richer-recovery choice consistently warns of possible Apex danger without revealing actual tier/species; rare quiet richer recoveries remain lucky outcomes and yield no creature loot without a defeated creature. There is no new combat escape mechanic.

Each wreck has at most one fight. Beating an approach ambusher opens all condition-appropriate salvage: picked-clean stays modest, uncertain gives its deeper haul, fresh its richer haul. There is no second fight or redundant quick/linger gamble after the threat is cleared. Empty departure forfeits that slot's earnings without an added resource deduction or replacement encounter.

Replace Search Hold / Strongbox / Timbers with one mixed haul appropriate to condition and outcome. Quick and deeper payouts do not stack. Successful deeper recovery guarantees actual wreck cargo, not a specific item or iron. Keep the defeated creature's full normal rewards separately, including its organic resources. Manufactured materials remain cargo, not monster-kill awards.

Small salvage retains a slim chance of rarer manufactured materials, including iron; risky/richer recovery has a much higher chance and better consumable odds. Gold may appear as a secondary cargo reward. Wreck cargo does not heal hull immediately: recovery comes through possible existing consumables. Exact contents, rates and quantities remain tuning work.

### Services, repairs and supplies

- **Midpoint Haven:** Choose one free careen/drill/forage activity, with paid upgrades and capped paid repairs available alongside it. Careening and paid work have separate capacities. No particular ordinary alternative is guaranteed.
- **Final repair/refit stop:** Paid upgrades and predictable bounded paid repairs, with no additional free heal. The proposed 30–50% maximum-hull capacity is an evaluation range, not a chosen value or a cap automatically copied to other services.
- **Final merchant:** Limited materials and consumables. Its identity is known when choosing a course, but exact quantities and consumable types are learned on arrival. It is distinct from the repair/refit specialist.
- **Wandering smuggler:** Retain paid healing in the working plan for now, using capped work; exact allowance/pricing and possible later removal remain balance questions. Current unrestricted repeat repairs are baseline evidence, not the target.
- **Post-Titan strongholds:** Keep their combined merchant, upgrade and repair services; no new stronghold repair cap is selected. Secluded coves retain their existing shore-activity role without adding a paid shipwright service.

For capped paid repairs, measure normal work before captain healing modifiers; bonuses/tradeoffs then change actual restoration, subject to missing hull. Fix the normal allowance from maximum hull at the first actual repair purchase. Later refits do not enlarge or replenish it, even after partial use; quotes/menu viewing alone do not lock it. Calculate careening when used, and keep it spent after upgrading. Explain the percentage basis and actual healing/cost clearly so upgrading first is an understandable strategy.

A hull refit adds equal fixed maximum/current HP and preserves existing absolute damage. Healing modifiers affect neither gain; the refit consumes no repair allowance. Drilling's accuracy benefit should prepare the next whole battle, survive intervening peaceful encounters and refresh rather than stack; exact potency remains open.

Allow deliberate outside-battle use of carried Hull Patch Kits, including during loot handling. A newly found kit can also be used directly without stowing it or discarding a belt item first. Consume only that kit for its normal healing; retaining it for later requires a slot. These changes neither guarantee kits nor increase potency/belt capacity. Normal inventory choices and relevant captain modifiers remain.

### Merchant stock and progression

Sea Glass and Depth-Wrought Iron join existing consumables at merchants, with separate finite material/supply allocations. Each merchant rolls its own bounded assortment and quantities. Purchases deplete that fixed shipment; reopening/reloading does not refresh it. Different merchants have separate stock, with no additional region/voyage purchase quota.

Pre-Titan merchants and both strongholds reliably start with bounded positive glass and iron stocks; ketch material presence is chance-based and may be absent. Material stock levels stay even across regions for a given merchant type, while individual shipments vary. Higher iron price is the principal selected distinction from glass; exact prices/distributions remain open. Later purchasing power may grow through the purse, not larger material shipments or approval of the old exponential income curve.

Limited trade should relieve pressure to visit every wreck without routinely giving a fresh lineage the entire blueprint roster. Purchased permanent materials retain their normal treatment through death. Gold remains transient. This is a deliberate spending tradeoff against repairs, refits and tactical supplies, not a promise to buy every material and still max the ship.

Working progression milestones are first fitting plus modest repairs after roughly two ordinary hunts, first hull plus repairs after roughly three, earlier specialization when prioritized, and full hull/fitting upgrades late in Region 3 while also paying repairs. These are representative targets, not hard gates, guaranteed purses or fixed purchase counts. The 40g bounty / 30g repair reserve example and earlier audit curves remain unselected values.

### Chart and fiction

Make the nautical chart part of navigation: a procedural ship route, dotted travelled line, visited-location markers and combat-outcome history. Show what actually happened, not a revealed future route. Port/starboard are short diversions toward discoveries before returning to the prevailing wind's broader Fontward course; they do not create loops, backtracking, safer lanes, extra encounters or distance-based costs. Early presentation must respect the Font's spoiler boundary.

Continued passage brings the ship into each regional Titan's reach, where it intercepts the captain; this explains why drawing a lateral route cannot bypass the boss. It does not invent an ocean-wide geographical barrier or turn Kraken/World Serpent into deliberate Font guardians. Exact chart layout, geometry, art, camera behavior and approach writing remain open. Flutter/Dart/Flame stays selected.

### Critical review and remaining work

The structure gives hunts a clearer finance role, manufactured-material alternatives and valuable identified matchups, while wrecks trade information/certainty for salvage. It preserves actual respite and bounded, budgeted preparation. It has **not** demonstrated that combat avoidance or wreck-heavy play stops dominating. A won wreck with the same creature and costs pays more than that matched hunt; unknown matchups, empty passes, occasional ambushes and quiet/quick recovery must be evaluated across whole journeys, not assumed to balance the extra cargo.

Also review combined careen + paid work + kits + refit capacity growth + stronghold repairs + triage, and forage-versus-careen spending. Outside-battle kit use reduces overflow waste and can raise effective recovery. Poor/no-cove/poor-kit routes, heavily damaged captains, merchant-rich routes, healing traits and capped late builds must retain understandable decisions. Full upgrade timing and blueprint access require separate fresh/progressed-profile budgets.

Remaining work is a bounded implementation brief with explicit starting parameters, state/reward ownership, save compatibility and verification, plus precise chart presentation. The offered/runtime difficulty mismatch is a known separate prerequisite for trustworthy previews and comparisons; choosing its correction/scaling is not approved by this review. Ambush rates, condition mix, committed Apex rates, cargo/stock quantities, prices, repair caps and drilling strength are not yet selected numbers. Exact Patch Kit controls, full-hull feedback and any merchant purchase-and-use shortcut likewise remain details to specify, not silently assumed rules.

#### Fresh-wreck quick search: design review

**Distinct interaction patterns reaffirmed by the user, 4 November 2026:** Retain A: picked-clean wrecks allow only quick small recovery; uncertain wrecks offer quick small salvage versus lingering; fresh wrecks offer richer guarded recovery or sailing past empty. Fresh wrecks have no quick small-salvage option. The user values approaching a wreck before assessing the actual recovery situation. Preserve broad distant condition clues as imperfect information and the normal-only approach ambush possibility; sailing past is the safer available choice once reached, not immunity to approach danger. The comparison below records alternatives considered, not equally active targets.

| Wreck condition | A — distinct interaction patterns | B — add a dangerous quick search at fresh wrecks |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Picked-clean | Only quick, small recovery | Same |
| Uncertain | Quick modest salvage and depart, or linger for richer salvage and high fight risk | Same |
| Fresh | Commit to the richer recovery or leave empty | Quick smaller recovery with high fight risk, or slower richer recovery with nearly certain combat; empty pass-by remains available |

Both retain occasional normal-only approach ambushes before recovery choices. For A, preserve the previously selected rare quiet fresh recovery: high combat likelihood does not become an unconditional fight guarantee. Both searches use the same encounter slot; no separate travel/time economy or shorter combat is selected.

**A — design and experience:** The conditions produce different decisions as well as different reward profiles. Picked-clean wrecks offer a modest recovery after approach danger, uncertain wrecks test how much to risk, and fresh wrecks ask whether this guarded opportunity is worth pursuing at all. The fresh choice has only one productive recovery action, but heading selection and empty departure still provide agency. A damaged captain may forfeit the opportunity to survive until a Haven, as already accepted. Readable condition clues help that loss feel understandable; inconclusive views can still produce an unlucky lost earning opportunity. This structure avoids expecting every wreck to provide a quick reward.

The main cost is less flexibility at fresh wrecks and the possibility that they feel close to a hunt with cargo attached. Species/Apex uncertainty, the recovery decision and rare quiet windfalls help distinguish them, but full creature rewards plus cargo still require a whole-outcome balance review. Distinct menus do not prove balance.

**B — design and experience:** This supports a thematic hurried grab near a recent sinking and another kind of risky decision. It is not a universal peaceful reward route: even its quick option commonly causes combat. It can create a memorable lucky escape or a deliberate gamble for a captain wanting something rather than leaving empty.

Its weak point is that quick salvage can incur almost the same full battle cost for less cargo. If both options frequently produce the same kind of fight, a healthy captain often favors the larger haul; a badly damaged captain already has an empty departure. Quick need not be equally attractive, and even a small chance of avoiding a lethal fight can matter, but its useful space can become narrow. Greater fight likelihood also means more chances to earn the creature's normal gold/organic rewards, so combat cannot be counted only as a penalty.
